01

The subsidy was going to the wrong party

Korea Commission for Corporate PartnershipGraduate hiring program redesign2020–2023

A government program meant to help small firms hire university graduates was barely being used, by either side. Rather than push harder on recruitment, I ran structured interviews with 30 participating companies and surveyed students to find out why.

Three causes came back. The program was not customized to individual firms. The value proposition was unclear from the applicant's side. And the subsidy went to the company rather than to the new hire, who was the one actually carrying the risk of joining a small firm. I redesigned around all three, piloted, then scaled.

30companies interviewed
246graduates hired
~5×versus prior results
What I took from it The instinct to diagnose before you act matters more than moving fast.
02

North Carolina's first statewide impact manager inventory

Duke CASE i3 Consulting PracticumClient: Good TRBLLed the Financial Inclusion workstreamSpring 2026

Seven students, one client, four focus areas. I owned Financial Inclusion end to end: the literature and policy research, outreach to 13 general partners, the interviews, and the written section that went to the client.

The database took 500+ raw entries down to 406 verified managers through a two-step screen. I declined to inherit the data provider's taxonomy. Not every investment in financial services should count as financial inclusion, so I narrowed the test to firms explicitly targeting underserved populations or structural barriers to credit. That kept a good deal of impact-washing out of the final list. I also wrote the limits of my own dataset into the deliverable: being headquartered in North Carolina does not mean a firm invests there.

31%had disclosed, verifiable AUM
13GPs contacted, 3 interviewed
The finding I did not expect I went in expecting capital scarcity. What came up, repeatedly, was the difficulty of translating interest into deployment. Capital wasn't the binding constraint. The connective tissue was.
03

33 US banks, one capability map, and a contact list I didn't trust

Ledger Ridge PartnersSummer Advisory Analyst, institutional digital assetsSummer 2026

Twelve Tier 1 banks, twenty Tier 2, and a standalone Bank of America diagnostic, all aligned to a client partner's three service lines so gaps were comparable across institutions. The Bank of America read was a gap diagnosis, not a market survey: the patents were a hedge, the research was positioning, the platform did not exist. A separate report on 22 asset managers inverted the thesis. Banks need infrastructure to launch products. Asset managers have already launched and need distribution and settlement rails.

Before outreach went out, I checked the contact list against its sources line by line. Nine of seventeen rows needed correcting. One person listed as a bank executive was in fact an equity research analyst at the group's securities arm. Anything I could not verify, I escalated rather than guessed.

04

One intake framework for four mandates that don't agree

S.Grand GroupM&A Fellowship, deal originationSummer 2026

Four acquirer mandates whose screens genuinely conflict. One works a revenue box, one an EBITDA floor, one an ownership structure, one a geography. I built a single twelve-field intake card all four run through, so the pipeline could be worked in parallel instead of four separate ways.

About 26 companies researched. When the first pass produced nothing inside one buyer's range, the answer was not to source harder. Every candidate had fallen below the revenue floor, so the screen was wrong. I rebuilt sourcing around that constraint and produced the first candidate inside the box. Disqualified targets got documented with the reason, so nobody would research them twice.

Written into the client deliverables "$77M–$118M (sources conflict, verify)." "Email addresses are estimated from company domain patterns. Verify before sending." Flagging my own uncertainty beats handing over a clean number I can't defend.
05

A project my own organization's norms said to stay out of

Korea Commission for Corporate PartnershipFormer mining region revitalization2020–2023

A region that had lost its economic base. Companies unwilling to absorb the cost and uncertainty of relocating there. Residents who wanted real compensation and doubted the companies were serious. The agency had always played a passive, facilitating role in this kind of project, and senior colleagues advised against doing more.

I took full ownership knowing failure would land on me. One-on-ones with executives to learn what each actually needed. A tailored incentive package built with government agencies. Town halls and workshops with residents. Then the part that mattered most: a standing collaboration committee, so the two sides negotiated directly and the project stopped depending on me sitting in the middle of it.

How I work

People before data

Thirty company interviews before redesigning the hiring program. GP and LP conversations before writing the financial inclusion section. One-on-ones with executives before proposing incentives. The insight has never once arrived from a spreadsheet first.

Distrust the category I was handed

The screen everyone accepts is usually the thing hiding the answer. A data provider's taxonomy would have let impact-washing through. When sourcing failed against a mandate, the screen was wrong, not the effort.

AI as a junior analyst, not an oracle

I build it into repeatable workflows: research pipelines, a project system that finished a client engagement ahead of schedule, an email triage dashboard. Then I ask where every claim came from and read the source myself. Numbers especially. Nothing goes out unchecked.

Leave behind something that runs without me

A compliance platform 500 companies use. An intake card four buyers run through. A standing committee that made my own mediation unnecessary. When you have no authority, a framework everyone agrees to is how you get leverage.
