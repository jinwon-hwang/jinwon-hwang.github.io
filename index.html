Graduate hiring program redesign

A government program meant to help small firms hire university graduates was barely being used, by either side. Rather than push harder on recruitment, I ran structured interviews with 30 participating companies and surveyed students to find out why.

Three causes came back. The program was not customized to individual firms. The value proposition was unclear from the applicant's side. And the subsidy went to the company rather than to the new hire, who was the one actually carrying the risk. I redesigned around all three, piloted, then scaled.

I do not just use AI. I built the loop that checks it.

Everyone claims AI fluency now. What has actually mattered in my work is the opposite of enthusiasm: deciding where the output does not get trusted, and making that check its own deliverable.

The loop

I ask for the source, read the source myself, and write the first draft before handing anything over for critique. When a number cannot be traced I escalate it instead of filling it in. The output gets treated the way you would treat a junior analyst's work: useful, and not trusted until it is checked.

Six steps, the same on every project. The one people find odd is that I write the draft first.

What the loop caught

Nobody asked me to re-check the Tier 2 contact list. I ran all seventeen rows against their sources anyway, before outreach went out. Nine needed fixing. One name filed as a bank executive turned out to be an equity research analyst at the group's securities arm, a completely different job.

The result is something that did not happen, which is why it lives here and not on a resume.

Built, not just used

Six working systems, none of them with an engineering background behind them: email triage, a research pipeline, a weekly scan that updates its own tracking files, and the project system that carried a seven-person client practicum in ahead of schedule.

Taught myself, partly on YouTube, partly by making the model explain its own code back to me.

How I work

People before data

Thirty company interviews before redesigning the hiring program. GP and LP conversations before writing the financial inclusion section. One-on-ones with executives before proposing incentives. The insight has never once arrived from a spreadsheet first.

Distrust the category I was handed

The screen everyone accepts is usually the thing hiding the answer. Pitchbook's taxonomy would have let impact washing through, so I narrowed it. One buyer's revenue floor was disqualifying the whole pipeline, so I rebuilt the screen rather than the effort.

Write down what I cannot verify

"Sources conflict, verify" shows up inside documents a client reads, not only in my notes. On one target the revenue ran from $15M to $175M across three databases, so I wrote that down rather than pick one and have to defend it later.

Ship the structure, not the memo

A compliance platform 500 companies file through. An intake card four buyers run through. A committee that made my own mediation unnecessary. The framework is what gets handed over, which is why it keeps working after I leave.
